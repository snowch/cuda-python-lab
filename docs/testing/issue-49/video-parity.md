# From the first-value recording to a learner-controlled experiment

The recording follows one float32 value from CPU allocation to a device write and back. Its counterexample is that two arrays can contain zero without referring to the same element. The interactive exercise preserves that example and lets the learner test it with different starting values.

The review used the authored narration and speech script, the shipped English captions, and decoded frames from the shipped recording. FFmpeg decoded the entire audio and video streams. The contact sheet samples all seven stages. In an offline Chromium session the retained player decoded the video, sought to the device store and displayed its corresponding timed caption. The MP4 is 79.37 seconds, H.264 3840 × 2160, with AAC audio and a timed-text track. **What changed pedagogically.** The learner controls time, direction and entry point. Selecting a buffer explains its role even before it exists. The optional hardware note separates logical allocations from physical backing on a discrete or integrated GPU. There is no animation timer, forced prediction, inferred copy time or invented device observation. Free editing remains available throughout. The exact-source check withdraws the model if the learner goes beyond its examples.

**Parity decision.** Each narrated learning point has an interactive counterpart. The recorded voice and its pacing are still available for learners who prefer to listen. An independent reviewer and learner evaluation can judge instructional equivalence. Keep the video in this release. Following independent acceptance, a separately reviewed removal of the MP4 could save 2,992,665 uncompressed offline bytes.
